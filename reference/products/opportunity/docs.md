# Product Brief: Seer Markets End to End

## What it is
- Seer is a prediction market platform that leverages Reality.eth and uses the Conditional Tokens Framework to create and manage markets. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
- Market types named in the facts: **Categorical** markets let users select one outcome out of multiple options; **Multi-categorical** markets allow selection of multiple outcomes; **Scalar** markets have a numeric outcome within a specified range; **Futarchy** markets are used to make decisions based on a token’s predicted price. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
- An **outcome token** is a digital asset representing a specific outcome in a prediction market; each token corresponds to a particular result and can be bought, sold, or traded. Prices are not fixed—they move with the market’s perceived likelihood of that outcome occurring. Outcome tokens are only convertible into the underlying asset once the market question is resolved. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
- Outcome tokens are represented as **ERC1155** tokens within the Conditional Tokens system; Seer wraps ERC1155 outcome tokens into **ERC20** tokens to improve usability and easier trading/integration with existing DeFi protocols. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
- Outcome tokens are backed by collateral, typically a stablecoin, which guarantees winners can be paid at resolution. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
- Every Seer market automatically carries an **invalid outcome**, used when no predefined outcome accurately reflects the real result. If a market resolves as invalid, invalid outcome tokens are redeemable for the underlying collateral. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]

## Who uses it and why
- **Traders / users betting on outcomes:** to bet on an outcome, a user first needs to hold outcome tokens. Outcome tokens serve two purposes: they can be traded, or redeemed for collateral tokens. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
- **Liquidity providers:** can provide liquidity for any outcome they choose and earn a proportion of the trading fees generated by the outcome they support. Liquidity provision works through liquidity pools, where providers earn a small fee on each trade executed in their pool. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity.md] [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
- **Market creators:** Seer markets must follow platform guidelines; creators are instructed to read the **Verified Markets Policy** hosted on Kleros IPFS and a separate **Market Resolution Policy** before creating a market. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]
- **AI agents / AI markets:** the docs note an existing experiment in which AI agents participate in prediction markets. The stated main potential of AI agent markets is high-speed, algorithm-to-algorithm participation where humans cannot practically participate. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/applications/ai-markets.md]
- AI-market applications named in the facts include: predicting whether a user would interact with particular content; predicting the star rating a user would give to a business such as a shop or restaurant on a map; predicting stars given to content on streaming platforms; predicting whether a user would click an ad or make a purchase related to an ad; and predicting whether users on a dating app would match and have a sustained interaction. The proposed AI-market mechanisms are said to apply across many domains. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/applications/ai-markets.md]
- In the social-network example: AIs submit candidate content with a very small amount of money used as liquidity for a market on whether a user will like that content; AIs then trade on those content markets; the content with the highest “Yes” prediction is displayed while other markets are cancelled; after the user interacts, markets resolve and AIs profit or lose based on whether their prediction was correct; AIs that proposed content and supplied liquidity for content the user liked receive a small reward. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/applications/ai-markets.md]
- The facts also describe machine learning primarily as a prediction technology, with examples such as ad clicks and movie preferences, and describe the current internet as dominated by services using AI to predict user behaviour; if decentralized protocols replace corporate predictive engines, prediction markets are proposed as the open replacement process. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/applications/ai-markets.md]

## Core mechanism (step by step)

1. **Create a market**
   - Market creation is initiated from the home page via a **“Create a market”** button. The first step is selecting a market type; the second is entering the market question and its outcomes; the third is specifying an opening date; the fourth is previewing the market, after which the creator clicks **Create Market**. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]
   - Multi-scalar markets require a specific question format, with the outcome type placed in brackets in place of the outcome value; the example references a party-name placeholder within a 2024 U.S. Presidential Election question. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]
   - Resolution of a market is only possible after its opening date has passed. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]
   - Markets can be verified at creation time using a switch button in the **“Verify it Now”** row, or verification can alternatively be completed after the market is created. Unverified markets display a warning on the platform. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]

2. **Prepare the market condition and outcome tokens**
   - Each market condition is prepared using the Conditional Tokens contract. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
   - Seer supports multiple Reality.eth question templates, including single-select, multi-select, and numeric scalar questions. Reality.eth questions are submitted with parameters such as opening time, timeout, and minimum bond. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
   - Outcome tokens are ERC1155 tokens within the Conditional Tokens system; Seer wraps ERC1155 outcome tokens into ERC20 tokens to improve usability. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]

3. **Acquire outcome tokens by minting / splitting**
   - Minting is also called **splitting**. Minting requires the user to hold available collateral, either **sDAI** or **xDAI**, and returns outcome tokens to the user. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
   - Split (minting) converts one collateral token into a complete set of outcome tokens for a market, one token per outcome. A mint always produces a full set of outcome tokens, not only the outcome the user wants to bet on. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
   - Minting is a 1:1 split against sDAI: 1 sDAI mints 1 outcome token of every outcome. Because of the sDAI/xDAI exchange rate, 1 xDAI mints roughly 0.9 outcome tokens per outcome. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
   - Outcome tokens can also be bought or sold from a token pool if enough liquidity exists, in addition to minting. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens.md]

4. **Trade and price outcome tokens**
   - Outcome token prices are not fixed—their value moves with the market’s perceived likelihood of that outcome occurring. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
   - Seer’s practical implementation has two components: an **AMM** and an **auction system**. Liquidity providers interact with the AMM contract, while traders interact with the auction contract. The AMM restricts trading to the auction contract only. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/seer-solution/practical-implementation-amm-and-auctions.md]
   - A trader buying an outcome token sends underlying tokens such as a stablecoin or sDAI to the auction contract. The auction contract then trades with the AMM, immediately updating the AMM price. After the AMM trade, the auction contract owns the outcome tokens being bought and auctions them. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/seer-solution/practical-implementation-amm-and-auctions.md]
   - The auction begins with the original trader as current winner and the bid price equal to what that trader paid. If no one overbids, the original trader receives the outcome tokens, making the system function like a classic AMM with a small settlement delay. At the end of the bidding period, the winner receives the outcome tokens. Any extra underlying tokens from overbidding are sent to the AMM and added to liquidity provider rewards. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/seer-solution/practical-implementation-amm-and-auctions.md]
   - Seer uses a bounded linear AMM because outcome token prices are bounded between 0 and 1. There is a minimum bid increment, for example 0.1%, to prevent trivial base-unit overbidding. Selling outcome tokens uses a descending auction where traders bid to accept the lowest amount of underlying tokens. [https://docs.seer.pm/whitepaper/seer-solution/practical-implementation-amm-and-auctions.md]
   - In the app buy flow, outcome choices are on the left and the collateral amount entry is on the right, with a **Buy** button. Clicking Buy opens a confirmation popup for the trade; the trade confirmation shows details including price and fee; the **Continue** button executes the trade after confirmation. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens.md]
   - Trades can experience slippage between expected and actual received amounts because trading occurs on a DEX. A minimum received amount protects users from large price spikes; otherwise the trade reverts. Selling is the reverse of buying: it swaps outcome tokens back into collateral tokens. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens.md]
   - The maximum trading price for an outcome token is 1 sDAI per token, so buying is disabled above that to prevent definite losses. If the liquidity pool lacks enough tokens, the app can block purchases that would cost more than 1 sDAI per outcome token. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens.md]

5. **Provide liquidity**
   - To provide liquidity, a user first visits a market, selects an outcome, and mints outcome tokens. Each outcome has an **Add liquidity** button in the Seer interface. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity.md]
   - For markets without liquidity, users are directed to the Swapr site. For markets that already have liquidity, a popup appears and clicking **Open** directs the user to Swapr; on Swapr, users should check that the selected pair is correct. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity.md]
   - Normally liquidity is provided in sDAI, but conditional markets use two outcome tokens: the pair consists of a conditional outcome token and the parent outcome token. Providing liquidity for a token pair requires holding both tokens. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity.md]
   - Users set an initial price for the pair, which determines how much of each token must be provided. Swapr uses concentrated liquidity, letting LPs provide tokens within a specific price range; LPs earn their fee share only within the active liquidity range. Users unsure about range selection can click **Full Range**. Swapr calculates the required token ratio from the initial price and selected range, and users must provide both tokens in that ratio. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity.md]

6. **Resolve**
   - Seer uses Reality.eth as its oracle system for resolving market outcomes, and uses Kleros to resolve disputes that may arise when answering Reality.eth questions. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
   - A Seer market can be resolved by waiting until the current reported answer becomes final, or a user can raise a dispute and wait for a ruling before resolution. After the relevant status appears, the user clicks the **Report Answer** button to resolve the market. The resolution UI includes a **Report Answer** action and displays a status before resolution. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market.md]
   - Disagreement or ambiguity about the correct outcome triggers a resolution dispute, handled by a predefined dispute resolution mechanism. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
   - Every market automatically carries an invalid outcome; if a market resolves as invalid, invalid outcome tokens are redeemable for the underlying collateral. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
   - Scalar markets cover a continuous range and pay proportionally: DOWN tokens redeem fully at/below the minimum, UP tokens fully at/above the maximum, and both partially in between. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
   - Conditional markets only resolve if a condition tied to a parent market occurs, and their outcome tokens convert into parent outcome tokens only if that condition holds. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]

7. **Merge / redeem**
   - Merge is the inverse of split: a complete set of outcome tokens is combined back into a single collateral token. Merging converts a complete set of outcome tokens back into collateral (xDAI or sDAI) and requires a prior token approval allowing Seer to handle the user’s outcome tokens. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
   - Redemption exchanges winning outcome tokens for collateral, normally at a 1:1 ratio, after resolution. Redemption is only possible after the market has been resolved; the redeem flow mirrors the merge flow in the UI. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
   - Once a market is resolved, holders can redeem their winning outcome tokens. In the example, Seer outcome tokens are referred to as **YES tokens** for the winning outcome. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market.md]

## Lifecycle/states
- **Created / unverified or verified:** markets can be verified at creation time with **Verify it Now**, or after creation; unverified markets display a warning. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]
- **Opening date not passed / resolution not possible:** resolution of a market is only possible after its opening date has passed. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market.md]
- **Resolution pending / status displayed:** the resolution UI displays a status before resolution and includes a **Report Answer** action. A market can be resolved by waiting until the current reported answer becomes final, or by raising a dispute and waiting for a ruling. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market.md]
- **Dispute:** disagreement or ambiguity about the correct outcome triggers a resolution dispute handled by a predefined dispute resolution mechanism; Kleros resolves disputes that may arise when answering Reality.eth questions. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md] [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/what-is-seer.md]
- **Resolved:** once resolved, holders can redeem winning outcome tokens. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market.md]
- **Invalid:** every market automatically carries an invalid outcome; if resolved as invalid, invalid outcome tokens are redeemable for the underlying collateral. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
- **Redeem UI states:** the docs distinguish two UI states for the redeem flow based on market resolution; redemption is only possible after the market has been resolved. [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens.md]
- **Conditional market state:** conditional markets only resolve if a condition tied to a parent market occurs, and outcome tokens convert into parent outcome tokens only if that condition holds. [https://docs.seer.pm/documentation/glossary.md]
