# concepts mechanism (home docs; DeepSeek, from quote-verified facts)

## What Seer is

Seer is a live production prediction-market protocol on mainnet at `seer.pm`; the app is at `https://app.seer.pm/` [https://docs.seer.pm] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/navigate-our-site]. A prediction market lets participants buy shares of future outcomes; shares are tokenized/tradable—e.g., ERC20—and each share redeems for **$1 if its outcome occurs, $0 otherwise** [https://docs.seer.pm]. The docs use a 2028 presidential election with named outcome shares as the canonical example, and state that a share price maps directly to an implied probability percentage [https://docs.seer.pm]. The stated justification is that prediction markets match or beat expert opinion and estimate aggregation; the problem framing is that prior prediction markets remained niche; delivery is incremental, and methods may be temporary or mutually exclusive with testing deciding what to keep [https://docs.seer.pm]. The Introduction page is an extract of the Seer whitepaper, and the paper’s two scoped questions define the product thesis, but those two questions are **not listed in the provided facts** [https://docs.seer.pm]. Documentation separates app usage from building [https://docs.seer.pm].

App-level capabilities include depositing collateral, creating markets, trading outcome tokens, and providing liquidity [https://docs.seer.pm]. Users can deposit wxDAI or sDAI; deposit-token docs cover Ethereum and Gnosis [https://docs.seer.pm/app/navigate-our-site].

## Roles and incentives

- **Traders/bettors:** buy or sell outcome tokens from a token pool when liquidity is sufficient; profit comes from trading P/L and redemption of winning shares at $1 [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens] [https://docs.seer.pm]. The leaderboard ranks wallets by trading profit and loss in USD across Seer markets, with P/L, volume, ROI, traded markets, and Trader Score [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard].
- **Market creators:** create markets from the home page by selecting type, entering question/outcomes, setting an opening date, previewing, and clicking Create Market; creators are directed to read the Verified Markets Policy and Market Resolution Policy first [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. Verification adds the market to the Curate List of Verified Markets and is intended to give extra credibility; verified markets must meet strict criteria, and verification requires images for both the market and its outcomes [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. Creators need sufficient xDAI for verification; the deposited xDAI is returned once the market is verified [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. **No explicit creator fee or monetary creator incentive is stated in the provided facts.**
- **Liquidity providers:** can provide liquidity for any outcome of their choice and earn a proportion of the fees generated from trading that outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. On Swapr, concentrated liquidity lets users provide within a specific price range, but LPs earn their fee share only within the active liquidity range [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].
- **Reality.eth reporters/answerers:** after opening time, users can report an answer to Reality; they need enough bond—example: 0.1 DAI—and later answerers must pay at least twice as much bond as the previous answerer [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. A final answer, or an answer matching the final answer, returns the bond deposit plus bonds from incorrect earlier answers; if the answer is not final or does not match the final answer, the user loses their bonds [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer].
- **Disputers/appeals:** a dispute can be raised when the current bond required to submit another answer is too high; this submits the question to Kleros Court and requires a fixed arbitration fee set by Kleros [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. After a dispute request, the market no longer accepts new answers; users can submit evidence; a dispute typically takes up to 14 days and 12 hours, and an appeal adds 11 days per appeal with no evidence period [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute].
- **Oracle/arbitrator:** Seer uses Reality.eth as its oracle system for resolving market outcomes; Reality allows multiple players to provide answers decentralizedly, with the final correct answer rewarded [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. The arbitrator is the address that resolves Realitio disputes [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/marketfactory]. Kleros Court rules on disputes and the displayed answer is final after that [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute].
- **Developers/integrators:** the docs expose serverless functions for integrations/frontend/AI tools, an SDK (`@seer-pm/sdk`) with viem clients, and contract addresses via SDK helpers [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/api] [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/configuration]. Transaction history covers the requested account plus the TradeExecutor contracts it owns, because trades routed through an executor keep outcome tokens in that contract [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/api]. **No developer incentive is stated.**

## Step-by-step mechanism

### 1) Create a market
1. Start on the home page, select a market type, input the question and outcomes, specify an opening date, preview, then click Create Market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. A market can only be resolved after its opening date [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market].
2. Market types: categorical markets are tied to a Reality question with only one answer; multi-categorical markets are tied to a Reality question that can have one or more answers; scalar markets resolve to a numeric value; multi-scalar markets are tied to two or more Reality questions, each resolving to a numeric value [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/marketfactory]. Multi-scalar markets require a specific question-name format: each outcome has its own question, and the outcome type is placed in brackets in place of the outcome value [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market].
3. Validations/rules: `createCategoricalMarket` validates at least two outcomes; `createScalarMarket` validates upper and lower bounds and requires exactly two outcomes; `prepareCondition` requires an outcome-slot count that must not exceed **256**; Reality question encoding requires escaping special characters such as quotes [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/marketfactory]. The INVALID_RESULT outcome is excluded from `outcomes`, and `deployERC20Positions` names the INVALID_RESULT outcome **SER-INVALID** [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/market] [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/marketfactory].
4. MarketFactory creation follows four steps: ask Reality questions, prepare the condition, create ERC20 outcome tokens, and initialize a new market contract; the NewMarket event is emitted when a market is created [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/marketfactory]. `allMarkets` returns all markets created by the factory and `marketCount` returns the number created [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/marketfactory].
5. Optional verification: during creation, a switch in the “Verify it Now” row triggers a verification popup; verification can also be completed later [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. Unverified markets display a warning [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. Verified markets are shown with images and a Verified tag; markets you submitted that are verifying show images only to you [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market].

### 2) Acquire outcome tokens: mint/split or buy
- Betting requires outcome tokens; outcome tokens can be traded or redeemed for collateral tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens].
- Minting is also called splitting and requires available sDAI or xDAI; **1 sDAI mints 1 outcome token for each outcome**; due to the sDAI/xDAI exchange rate, **1 xDAI mints about 0.9 outcome tokens per outcome** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Minting returns outcome tokens for all outcomes, not only the selected outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens].
- Merge is the inverse of mint; merging exchanges outcome tokens for xDAI or sDAI and requires approving Seer to handle outcome tokens first [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Redeeming requires the market to be resolved first; the redeem flow is similar to merge [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens].
- Contract path: `GnosisRouter.splitFromBase` splits using xDAI and returns ERC20 outcome tokens; native xDAI is converted to sDAI and transferred to GnosisRouter, then Wrapped1155Factory wraps ERC1155 outcomes into ERC20 and sends them to the user [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/gnosisrouter]. `mergeToBase` unwraps ERC20 outcome tokens, burns them, returns ERC1155 tokens to GnosisRouter, converts sDAI to xDAI, and sends xDAI to the user [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/gnosisrouter]. `redeemToBase` redeems positions and sends xDAI; it requires `conditionalTokens.reportPayouts` to have been called by an oracle [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/gnosisrouter].
- Mainnet path: `splitFromDai` takes a Market and amount, transfers DAI from the user to MainnetRouter, converts to sDAI, splits the position, and Wrapped1155Factory wraps ERC1155 into ERC20 and returns to the user [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/mainnetrouter]. `mergeToDai` unwraps/burns ERC20 outcomes, returns ERC1155 to Router, converts sDAI to DAI, and sends DAI to the user [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/mainnetrouter]. For split/merge/redeem, each outcome’s ERC20 must already exist on Wrapped1155Factory [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/mainnetrouter].
- Conditional markets do not use sDAI for bets; users must first split or purchase parent outcome tokens from the main market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. In the Maduro example, the user needs “No” tokens from the parent market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. Minting/merging conditional outcome tokens uses parent outcome tokens instead of sDAI or xDAI, and follows the simple-market process [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. A conditional market can be nested and used like any market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets].
- Futarchy markets: creation starts on the Futarchy page via “Create new Proposal”; creators choose collateral tokens identified as token and currency; after preview, Create Market deploys a FutarchyProposal contract at a new address [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. A market generates four outcome tokens: YES_GNO, NO_GNO, YES_wstETH, and NO_wstETH; they trade in two liquidity pools called the yes pool and no pool [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Futarchy markets are currently available only on Gnosis Chain [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. To obtain conditional tokens, users first split either the token or the currency underlying; minting can use either the token or currency underlying, and merging returns outcome tokens to either the token or currency underlying depending on holdings [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. After resolution, users redeem token and/or currency underlying instead of sDAI or xDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].
- ConditionalTokens rule: a condition ID may be derived from oracle, questionId, and outcomeSlotCount; outcomeSlotCount must not exceed **256**; `reportPayouts` is called by the oracle; `splitPosition` reverts if the partition is trivial, invalid, or refers to more slots than the condition is prepared with [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/interaction/conditional-tokens/conditionaltokens]. Position IDs are constructed from a collateral token and an outcome collection and used as ERC-1155 IDs [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/interaction/conditional-tokens/conditionaltokens].

### 3) Buy or sell outcome tokens
1. Users can buy or sell outcome tokens from a token pool when there is enough liquidity [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].
2. Buy flow: select an outcome on the left, enter the amount on the right, then click Buy [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].
3. On Gnosis, the switch shows wxDAI or xDAI on the right depending on which token the user holds more of, while sDAI is always shown on the left; on Ethereum Mainnet, DAI is always shown on the right [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].
4. If there are not enough tokens in the liquidity pool, the interface may display a specific message; that insufficient-liquidity message means users cannot spend more than **1 sDAI to buy 1 outcome token**, and the example requires **15.38 sDAI for 1 YES token** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Buying is disabled when the maximum tradable price would exceed **1 sDAI per outcome token**, to prevent definite losses [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].
5. After clicking Buy, a confirmation popup appears and lets users check trade details including price and fee [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Because trading occurs on a DEX, there is slippage between expected and actual received amounts; a minimum received amount protects users from a huge price spike and causes a revert otherwise [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Clicking Continue executes the trade [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].
6. Selling is the reverse of buying: it swaps outcome tokens back into collateral tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].
7. Futarchy trading differs: on the market page, YES and NO tokens trade directly with each other rather than against shared collateral like sDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Futarchy markets have no invalid tokens; an invalid resolution automatically means the proposal failed [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].

### 4) Provide liquidity
1. Users can provide liquidity for any outcome; LPs earn a proportion of fees generated from trading that outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].
2. Flow: visit a market, select an outcome, mint outcome tokens; each outcome displays an Add liquidity button [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. If a market has no liquidity, users are directed to Swapr; if it already has liquidity, a popup appears and Open directs users to Swapr [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].
3. On Swapr, check the selected pair is correct; normally liquidity is provided in sDAI, but in conditional markets both tokens are outcome tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. Providing liquidity for a token pair requires holding both tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].
4. The initial price number determines the corresponding amount of each token to provide [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. Swapr uses concentrated liquidity; LPs earn their fee share only within the active liquidity range; if unsure, click Full Range [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. In “Enter amounts,” Swapr calculates the token ratio from the initial price and selected range, and both tokens must be provided in the correct ratio [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. After approval, clicking Add liquidity and confirming the transaction makes the user an LP on Swapr [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].

### 5) Report, dispute, and resolve
1. After the opening time, users can report their answer to Reality; the UI flow begins by clicking Answer on Reality.eth, inputting and submitting the answer [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. A user must have enough bond in their wallet; the example requires depositing **0.1 DAI** to submit an answer [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer].
2. Additional answers can be submitted after the first; a later answerer must pay at least **twice as much bond** as the previous answerer [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. If no other answer is submitted after a certain period, the market can be resolved [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. The exact length of that “certain period” is **not stated**.
3. A final answer, or an answer matching the final answer, returns the bond deposit plus bonds from incorrect earlier answers; if a user’s answer is not final or does not match the final answer, they lose their bonds [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer].
4. Dispute: when the current bond required to submit another answer is too high, a dispute can be raised; raising a dispute submits the question to Kleros Court and requires a fixed fee set by Kleros [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. After the dispute request, the market no longer accepts new answers; market status is updated once Kleros Court is notified [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. The UI provides “Check the Case on Kleros Court,” shows current stage and anticipated waiting period, and allows evidence submission [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. A dispute typically takes up to **14 days and 12 hours**; an appeal returns to voting and adds **11 days** per appeal, with no evidence period [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. The exact Kleros fee amount and exact per-stage durations are **not stated**.
5. Resolve: users can wait for the current answer to become final before resolving, or raise a dispute and wait for a ruling [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market]. After the appropriate status appears, the user can click Report Answer to resolve the market; the resolved state is “The market is resolved.” [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market]. After resolution, winning tokens can be redeemed; YES tokens are shown as an example of winning tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market].
6. Oracle contract logic: resolution requires the market’s Reality questions to be finalized, including any reopened question instead; the resolve flow hashes back the original questionId from questionsIds, outcomes.length, templateId, lowerBound, and upperBound, then calls one of four internal resolution functions for the four market types [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/realityproxy]. The categorical resolver resolves to invalid if the answer is invalid or the result is greater than the number of outcomes; categorical resolution creates a payouts array of length `numOutcomes+1`, with the last index for INVALID [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/realityproxy]. The multi-categorical resolver resolves to invalid if the answer is invalid or all results are zero [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/realityproxy].
7. Futarchy resolution: `FutarchyRealityProxy.resolve` requires the proposal’s Reality questions to be finalized, with reopened questions finalized instead; resolution logic accepts the proposal when the answer is **0**, and rejects for any other answer, including INVALID [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/futarchy/futarchyrealityproxy]. A proposal passes if the market predicts **YES_GNO/YES_wstETH will exceed NO_GNO/NO_wstETH** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].
8. Conditional resolution: a conditional market is resolved in the normal way; after resolution, conditional outcome tokens redeem for parent outcome tokens, which can then be redeemed in the parent market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. If the parent resolves differently from the child’s assumed outcome, the child and nested markets become obsolete and tokens redeem for zero [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets].

### 6) Redeem
After resolution, winning tokens can be redeemed [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market]. Redeeming requires the market to be resolved first; the redeem flow is similar to merge [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Conditional outcome tokens redeem for parent outcome tokens and then the parent; if the parent resolves differently, child/nested tokens redeem for zero [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. Futarchy users redeem token and/or currency underlying instead of sDAI or xDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Router redemption requires `reportPayouts` to have been called by an oracle; redeem return amounts are proportional to ConditionalTokens payouts, and a redeem call can process without caller tokens but has no effect [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/core/router].

### 7) Leaderboard and Trader Score rules
The leaderboard ranks wallets by trading P/L in USD across Seer markets; each wallet row includes P/L, volume, ROI, traded markets, and a Trader Score [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Wallets with no P/L, volume, capital at risk, and traded markets in the selected filters are omitted rather than shown as zero rows [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard].

Trader Score is a **0–100** rating derived from a wallet’s per-market P/L distribution; it is the **weighted mean of five components**, and clicking any score shows the five components, each raw value, and points contributed [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Hit-rate edge is scored against the break-even rate implied by the wallet’s own realized payoffs; small samples are shrunk toward 50 using **n ÷ (n + 10)**; hit rate carries **five pseudo-markets at 50%**, so **three wins out of three reads about 69%, not 100%** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. A wallet shows **N/A** when it has fewer than **3** markets with more than **$1** at risk, less than **$100** capital at risk, or more than **25%** of P/L outside those markets; N/A means too little history to judge, not a judged score of 0 [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Traded Markets counts every market touched, while scoring only counts markets with more than **$1** at risk; counts are per trader, not per address—a wallet and its TradeExecutor count once [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Scores are recalculated for the selected period, app, and chain filters [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard].

API-level tiers: Elite **≥85**, Great **≥70**, Good **≥55**, Average **≥40**, Weak below [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/api]. P/L periods are **1d, 1w, 1m, all**, defaulting to **1d** on the portfolio P/L endpoint [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/api]. P/L leaderboard app scopes are **all** (protocol-wide), **foresight**, **opportunity**, **deepfund**, or **futarchy** [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/api]. The exact weights and full definitions of all five Trader Score components are **not stated** in the provided facts.
