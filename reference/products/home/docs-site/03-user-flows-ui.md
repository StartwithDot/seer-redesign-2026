# user flows ui (home docs; DeepSeek, from quote-verified facts)

## User-facing flows and UI behaviors described

### Create a market
The create-market flow starts on the home page [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. Creators first select a market type, enter the question and outcomes, and for multi-scalar markets use a specific question-name format: each outcome has its own question, with the outcome type placed in brackets in place of the outcome value [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. An opening date must be specified, and the market can only be resolved after that date [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. Users preview the market, then click **Create Market** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market]. Creators are directed to read the Verified Markets Policy and Market Resolution Policy before creating a market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market].

Verification can be started during creation via a switch in the **Verify it Now** row, or completed later [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. During creation, clicking that switch triggers a verification popup [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. Verification requires submitting images for both the market and its outcomes, and requires sufficient xDAI; the deposited xDAI is returned once the market is verified [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. Verified markets show images and a **Verified** tag, while unverified markets display a warning [https://docs.seer.pm/app/create-a-market] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market]. Markets you submitted that are verifying show images visible only to you [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market].

### Trade outcome tokens: buy/sell
Users can buy or sell outcome tokens from a token pool when there is enough liquidity [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. The buy flow starts by selecting an outcome on the left, entering the amount on the right, then clicking **Buy** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. On Gnosis, the switch shows wxDAI or xDAI on the right depending on which token the user holds more of, while sDAI is always shown on the left; on Ethereum Mainnet, DAI is always shown on the right [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].

If the pool lacks enough tokens, the interface may display a specific insufficient-liquidity message [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. That message means users cannot spend more than 1 sDAI to buy 1 outcome token; the example requires 15.38 sDAI for 1 YES token [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Buying is disabled when the maximum tradable price would exceed 1 sDAI per outcome token, to prevent definite losses [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].

After clicking **Buy**, a confirmation popup appears showing trade details including price and fee [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Because trading occurs on a DEX, there is slippage between expected and actual received amounts; a minimum received amount protects against a huge price spike and causes a revert otherwise [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Clicking **Continue** executes the trade [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Selling is the reverse: it swaps outcome tokens back into collateral tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens].

### Mint, merge, redeem outcome tokens
Prediction-market betting requires outcome tokens, which can be traded or redeemed for collateral tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Minting is also called splitting and requires available sDAI or xDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. 1 sDAI mints 1 outcome token for each outcome; due to the sDAI/xDAI exchange rate, 1 xDAI mints about 0.9 outcome tokens per outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Minting returns outcome tokens for all outcomes, not only the selected outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens].

Merge is the inverse of mint and exchanges outcome tokens for xDAI or sDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Merging requires approving Seer to handle outcome tokens first [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Redeeming requires the market to be resolved first; the redeem flow is similar to merge, and the docs label unresolved and resolved market states in the redeem flow [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens].

### Provide liquidity
Users can provide liquidity for any outcome of their choice, and liquidity providers earn a proportion of the fees generated from trading that outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. The flow starts by visiting a market, selecting an outcome, and minting outcome tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. Each outcome displays an **Add liquidity** button [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. If a market has no liquidity, users are directed to Swapr; if it already has liquidity, a popup appears and **Open** directs users to Swapr [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. On Swapr, users should check that the selected pair is correct [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].

Normally liquidity is provided in sDAI, but in conditional markets both tokens are outcome tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. Providing liquidity for a token pair requires holding both tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. The initial price number determines the corresponding amount of each token, and Swapr uses concentrated liquidity so users can provide within a specific price range; liquidity providers earn their fee share only within the active range [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. If unsure, users can click **Full Range** [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. In **Enter amounts**, Swapr calculates the token ratio from the initial price and selected range, and both tokens must be provided in the correct ratio [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. After approval, clicking **Add liquidity** and confirming the transaction makes the user an LP on Swapr [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].

### Conditional markets
A conditional market can be created based on the outcome of an existing market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. Each outcome has a **New conditional market** link for creating a child market; the link opens the usual market form [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. The market form and the market page both show an alert that the market is conditional and depends on another market’s outcome [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets].

Conditional markets do not use sDAI for bets; users must first split or purchase parent outcome tokens from the main market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. In the Maduro example, the user needs “No” tokens from the parent market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. Conditional markets can be nested based on another conditional market and used like any market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. Minting and merging conditional outcome tokens follows the simple-market process but uses parent outcome tokens instead of sDAI or xDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. A conditional market is resolved in the normal way; after resolution, conditional outcome tokens redeem for parent outcome tokens, which can then be redeemed in the parent market [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. If the parent resolves differently from the child’s assumed outcome, the child and nested markets become obsolete and tokens redeem for zero [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets].

### Futarchy markets
Futarchy markets are price-based: participants trade on expectations about how decisions will affect a token’s price [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. A proposal passes if the market predicts YES_GNO/YES_wstETH will exceed NO_GNO/NO_wstETH [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Futarchy markets are currently available only on Gnosis Chain [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].

Creation starts on the Futarchy page via a **Create new Proposal** action [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Creators choose collateral tokens, identified as the token and the currency [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. A market generates four outcome tokens: YES_GNO, NO_GNO, YES_wstETH, and NO_wstETH, and these trade in two liquidity pools called the yes pool and no pool [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. The creator specifies an opening date, and the market can only resolve after that date [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. After previewing, **Create Market** deploys a FutarchyProposal contract at a new address [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].

On the market page, YES and NO tokens trade directly with each other rather than against shared collateral like sDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. To obtain conditional tokens, users first split either the token or the currency underlying [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Futarchy markets have no invalid tokens; an invalid resolution automatically means the proposal failed [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Minting outcome tokens can use either the token or the currency underlying, and merging returns outcome tokens to either the token or the currency underlying depending on holdings [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. After resolution, users redeem token and/or currency underlying instead of sDAI or xDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].

### Report an answer
Seer uses Reality.eth as its oracle system for resolving market outcomes [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. Users can report their answer to Reality after the opening time [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. The reporting UI flow begins by clicking **Answer** on Reality.eth; users input and submit their answer through the interface [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. A user must have enough bond in their wallet to submit an answer; the example requires depositing 0.1 DAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer].

Additional answers can be submitted after the first answer; a later answerer must pay at least twice as much bond as the previous answerer [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. If no other answer is submitted after a certain period, the market can be resolved [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer]. A final answer or an answer matching the final answer returns the bond deposit plus bonds from incorrect earlier answers; if a user’s answer is not final or does not match the final answer, they lose their bonds [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer].

### Resolve a market and raise a dispute
Users can wait for the current answer to become final before resolving, or raise a dispute and wait for a ruling [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market]. After the appropriate status appears, the user can click **Report Answer** to resolve the market; the resolved state is explicitly stated as “The market is resolved.” [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market]. After resolution, winning tokens can be redeemed, with YES tokens shown as an example [https://docs.seer.pm/app/resolve-market].

A dispute can be raised when the current bond required to submit another answer is too high [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. Raising a dispute submits the question to Kleros Court, and requesting arbitration requires a fixed fee set by Kleros [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. After a dispute request is submitted, the market no longer accepts new answers, and the market status is updated once Kleros Court is notified [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. The UI provides a **Check the Case on Kleros Court** action; the dispute view shows the current stage and anticipated waiting period, and users can submit evidence [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. A dispute typically takes up to 14 days and 12 hours to reach a decision; an appeal returns the dispute to the voting period and adds 11 days per appeal, and appeals do not include an evidence period [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute]. After Kleros rules, the market status is updated again and the displayed answer is final [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute].

### Leaderboard participation/viewing
The leaderboard ranks wallets by trading profit and loss in USD across Seer markets [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Each wallet row includes P/L, volume, ROI, traded markets, and a Trader Score [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Wallets with no P/L, volume, capital at risk, and traded markets in the selected filters are omitted rather than shown as zero rows [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Trader Score is a 0–100 rating derived from a wallet’s per-market profit and loss distribution and summarizes how profit was made, not just how much [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. It is the weighted mean of five components; hit rate edge is scored against the break-even rate implied by the wallet’s own realized payoffs, and small samples are shrunk toward 50 using n ÷ (n + 10) [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Hit rate carries five pseudo-markets at 50%, so three wins out of three reads about 69%, not 100% [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard].

A wallet shows N/A when it has fewer than 3 markets with more than $1 at risk, less than $100 capital at risk, or more than 25% of P/L outside those markets; N/A means too little history to judge, not a score of 0 [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Traded Markets counts every market touched, while scoring only counts markets with more than $1 at risk; counts are per trader, not per address, so a wallet and its TradeExecutor count once [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard]. Clicking any leaderboard score shows the five components, each raw value, and the points contributed; scores are recalculated for the selected period, app, and chain filters [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard].

## Prerequisites

- **App entry and navigation:** The Seer app URL is https://app.seer.pm/ [https://docs.seer.pm/app/navigate-our-site]. Documentation directs users to different entry points depending on intent: app usage vs building [https://docs.seer.pm]. Top-level navigation includes Seer, Whitepaper, Documentation, and Developers [https://docs.seer.pm].
- **Collateral deposit:** Users can deposit wxDAI or sDAI, and deposit-token documentation covers Ethereum and Gnosis [https://docs.seer.pm/app/navigate-our-site].
- **Chain-specific trading collateral:** On Gnosis, the buy/sell switch shows wxDAI or xDAI on the right depending on holdings, while sDAI is always on the left; on Ethereum Mainnet, DAI is always on the right [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Minting requires sDAI or xDAI, and merging returns xDAI or sDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens].
- **Conditional-market collateral:** Conditional markets do not use sDAI for bets; users must first split or purchase parent outcome tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets]. Minting/merging conditional outcome tokens uses parent outcome tokens instead of sDAI or xDAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/conditional-markets].
- **Futarchy collateral:** Creators choose collateral tokens identified as the token and the currency; minting/merging can use either the token or currency underlying, and redemption returns token and/or currency underlying [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets]. Futarchy markets are currently available only on Gnosis Chain [https://docs.seer.pm/app/futarchy-markets].
- **Approvals:** Merging requires approving Seer to handle outcome tokens first [https://docs.seer.pm/app/mint-merge-redeem-outcome-tokens]. Providing liquidity requires approval before clicking **Add liquidity** and confirming [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity]. The developer docs also provide an ERC20 approve ABI for collateral and outcome token approvals used in split, merge, redeem, and swaps [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/configuration].
- **Liquidity:** Buying/selling from a pool requires enough liquidity [https://docs.seer.pm/app/buy-sell-outcome-tokens]. Providing liquidity for a token pair requires holding both tokens [https://docs.seer.pm/app/provide-liquidity].
- **Reporting bond:** A user must have enough bond in their wallet to submit an answer; the example requires depositing 0.1 DAI [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer].
- **Dispute fee:** Requesting arbitration requires a fixed fee set by Kleros [https://docs.seer.pm/app/raise-a-dispute].
- **Verification collateral:** Verifying a market requires sufficient xDAI, returned once the market is verified [https://docs.seer.pm/app/verify-market].
- **Network footprint:** Deployed contracts are organized by Gnosis, Ethereum, Optimism, and Base [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/contracts/deployed-contracts]. The developer configuration says any chain from viem/chains such as gnosis, mainnet, and base can be used and examples are identical across networks [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/configuration].

**GAP — wallet prerequisite:** The provided facts do not describe a user-facing “connect wallet” screen or wallet-authentication prerequisite for the app. They mention wallets in the leaderboard, “enough bond in their wallet” for reporting, and a developer wallet client requiring a private key via `PRIVATE_KEY`, but not an app wallet-connection flow [https://docs.seer.pm/app/leaderboard] [https://docs.seer.pm/app/report-answer] [https://docs.seer.pm/developers/configuration].
